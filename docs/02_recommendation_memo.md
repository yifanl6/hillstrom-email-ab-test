# Memo: Which email should we send?

**To:** Marketing Lead  **Re:** Results of the 64,000-customer email test

## Recommendation
**Send the Men's merchandise email to the whole active customer file**, including customers who have only ever bought women's merchandise.

## What we tested
We split 64,000 recent customers at random into three equal groups. One group got the Men's email, one got the Women's email, and one got no email. We then tracked visits, purchases and spend for two weeks. The split was fair: group sizes and customer profiles matched almost exactly, so the differences below are caused by the emails.

## What we found
Both emails worked. The Men's email worked best on every measure.

| Per 10,000 customers emailed, extra vs. no email | Men's email | Women's email |
|---|---|---|
| Extra site visits | **+766** (about +72%) | +452 (about +43%) |
| Extra orders | **+68** | +31 |
| **Extra revenue (2 weeks)** | **$7,700** | $4,200 |
| Plausible range for extra revenue (95%) | $4,900 – $10,600 | $1,700 – $6,800 |

Without an email, 10,000 customers produce about 1,060 visits, 57 orders and $6,500 in revenue over two weeks. The Men's email roughly doubles orders and revenue.

The Men's email earns about **$3,500 more per 10,000 sends** than the Women's email. That gap is real but less certain: it could be anywhere from about $300 to $6,600.

## "Who should get it?"
We checked one specific worry in advance: does a Men's email turn off customers who only buy women's products? It does not. Their visits rose by 7 percentage points, from 9.6% to 16.6%, about the same as for everyone else. This check looked at visits only, so treat it as reassuring, not as final.

## What this does *not* tell you
- **Profit.** We have no data on the cost of sending emails or on product margins. Figures above are extra revenue, not extra profit. At these sizes, email costs are very unlikely to erase the gain.
- **Long-term effects.** We only saw two weeks. We don't know whether emails pull forward purchases that would have happened anyway, or whether repeated sends cause unsubscribes.
- **Today's customers.** The test ran in 2008. Inbox habits have changed, so a quick confirmation test with a holdout group is advisable before a full rollout.

**Suggested next step:** roll out the Men's email to 90% of the file and keep a 10% no-email holdout, to confirm the lift on current customers and measure unsubscribes.
